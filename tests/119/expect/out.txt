Noted Tuesday, February 10, 2015, in Winnipeg, and posted on the 
Yahoo! Groups WordStar group:

WordStar recognizes an undocumented ".wi" dot command:


It's a regular dot command, showing a "." in the flag column, not 
a "1," which indicates a dot command that works best at the top 
of a page, or a ":" for a merge-print dot command.  (And invalid 
dot commands show a "?" -- try ".wx" for instance; ".wi" is not 
invalid.)

My hope was that it was an undocumented "widow-orphan control" 
dot command, but it doesn't seem to do anything.  I tried it on 
its own and with arguments of:

 .wi 0
 .wi 1
 .wi 2
 .wi off
 .wi on
 .wi dis 
  
It's recognized in WordStar for DOS 4.0 right through to WordStar 
7.0.  

It's not for character-width; that's the ".cw" dot command.  And 
I doubt it stands for "WordStar International," since, as I said, 
the command is recognized in WordStar 4.0, and the company was 
known as MicroPro International back then.

Anyone know what it's for?  

Rob
